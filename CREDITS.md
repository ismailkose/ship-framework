# Credits

Ship learned from the people and projects below. Ideas are rewritten in Ship's own words and
checked against the official docs; text is reused only where the license allows it, with the
notice kept in the file.

## What Ship follows

- **[Emil Kowalski's skills](https://github.com/emilkowalski/skills)**: Ship's motion guidance
  follows them, from easing and springs to Reduce Motion and animation reviews. The MIT notice is
  kept in each file. *(MIT)*

## Ideas Ship learned from

- **[gstack](https://github.com/garrytan/gstack)** by Garry Tan: the AI team in your terminal,
  reviewers who start fresh and work side by side, and reviews tied to exactly what they saw. *(MIT)*
- **Paul Hudson's [SwiftData](https://github.com/twostraws/SwiftData-Agent-Skill),
  [Swift Concurrency](https://github.com/twostraws/Swift-Concurrency-Agent-Skill) and
  [Swift Testing](https://github.com/twostraws/Swift-Testing-Agent-Skill) skills**: saving data
  reliably, safer async code, and clearer tests. *(MIT)*
- **[swift-security-skill](https://github.com/ivan-magda/swift-security-skill)** by Ivan Magda:
  a checklist for keeping logins safe in the iOS keychain. *(MIT)*
- **[iOS Accessibility Agent Skill](https://github.com/dadederk/iOS-Accessibility-Agent-Skill)** by
  Daniel Devesa Derksen-Staats: Voice Control, Switch Control and Full Keyboard Access, and how to
  test them by hand. *(MIT)*
- **[web-quality-skills](https://github.com/addyosmani/web-quality-skills)** by Addy Osmani: check
  the rendered page, trace each problem to its code, fix it, and check again. *(MIT)*
- **[frontend-design](https://github.com/anthropics/skills)** by Anthropic: before showing a
  direction, ask whether another product would get the same one. *(Apache 2.0)*
- **[Jakub Krehel's skills](https://github.com/jakubkrehel/skills)**: one finding per root cause,
  listing every place it shows. *(MIT)*
- **[taste-skill](https://github.com/leonxlnx/taste-skill)** by leonxlnx: spotting the second
  default look models fall into when told to avoid the generic one. *(MIT)*
- **[design-skills](https://github.com/cuellarfr/design-skills)** by cuellarfr: contrast checks for
  text on buttons, focus rings, errors and charts. *(MIT)*
- **[agent-skills](https://github.com/vercel-labs/agent-skills)** by Vercel: a different look is a
  variant; a different behaviour is its own component. *(MIT)*
- **[userinterface.wiki](https://github.com/raphaelsalaja/userinterface-wiki)** by Raphael Salaja:
  pairing UX laws with what they mean in code. *(MIT)*
- **[V0Swift](https://github.com/krispuckett/V0Swift)** by Kris Puckett, and Vercel's post on
  building v0 for iOS: how a chat screen places messages and handles the keyboard. *(MIT; blog)*
- **[Growth.Design's cognitive biases and principles](https://growth.design/psychology)** by Dan
  Benoni and Louis-Xavier Lavallée: how people decide, used at the moment each principle matters,
  and the balance between helping people decide and tricking them.
- **Twelve Lenny's Podcast talks** by Katie Dill, Cat Wu, Claire Vo, Dan Shipper, Marty Cagan, Geoff
  Charles, Tamar Yehoshua, Elena Verna, Robby Stein, Ami Vora and Mike Krieger, Karri Saarinen, and
  Tara Sesha and Nan Yu: a product's point of view, the care review, learning from the people you
  build for, and features that earn their place.
- **Books and sites:** Practical UI (Adham Dannaway), Laws of UX (Jon Yablonski), the GOV.UK
  Design System, Jakob Nielsen's ten usability heuristics and response time limits, Practical
  Typography (Matthew Butterick), Microinteractions (Dan Saffer), The Elements of Typographic
  Style (Robert Bringhurst), Interaction of Color (Josef Albers), Space in Design Systems (Nathan
  Curtis), Nudge (Richard Thaler and Cass Sunstein), and NIST's password guidance.

## Tools Ship runs for you

Ship doesn't copy these. It runs or reads them at the moment they help, so you always get the
latest version.

- **[impeccable](https://github.com/pbakaus/impeccable)** by Paul Bakaus: when Ship reviews a web
  page, it runs this checker first to catch common design problems. *(Apache 2.0)*
- **[Web Interface Guidelines](https://github.com/vercel-labs/web-interface-guidelines)** by Vercel:
  in every web review, Ship reads the latest rules and checks your page against them. *(MIT)*
- **[DESIGN.md](https://github.com/google-labs-code/design.md)** by Google Labs: if you ask, Ship
  exports your design tokens in this shared format so other design tools can read them. *(Apache 2.0)*
- **[shadcn/ui](https://github.com/shadcn-ui/ui)**: in projects built with shadcn, Ship asks its own
  tool for the right component docs instead of guessing. *(MIT)*
